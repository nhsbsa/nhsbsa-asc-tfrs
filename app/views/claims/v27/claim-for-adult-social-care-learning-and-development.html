Claim for adult social care learning and development

Adult social care providers in England can claim reimbursement towards the cost of training their employees – check if you’re eligible and register your organisation.

From:
Department for Health and Social Care

Overview

This funding is available from the Learning and Development Support Scheme (LDSS). The LDSS aims to support adult social care staff to develop new skills by reducing training costs for their employers.

You’ll find more details in the LDSS guide for employers.

There are 2 separate ring-fenced funds available: one for Oliver McGowan Mandatory Training (OMMT) and another for all other courses and qualifications eligible under LDSS.

See a list of eligible training courses and qualifications.

Check if your organisation is eligible

To be eligible to submit claims your organisation needs to:

  • provide an adult social care service
  • directly employ care staff in England
  • have an up-to-date Adult Social Care Workforce Data Set (ASC-WDS) account

To create an ASC-WDS account, or check an existing one, visit the Skills for care website.

If you're planning to claim for OMMT

Your organisation also needs to be registered with the CQC as an adult social care provider to be eligible.

What you can claim for

To find out which courses and qualifications you can claim for check this list of eligible training courses and qualifications.

The list also shows the maximum reimbursement amounts available for each learner.

Who you can claim for

You can claim for:

  • staff who provide direct care and are not registered with a professional body
  • registered managers, including aspiring managers who are working towards registration

However, if you're claiming for Oliver McGowan Mandatory Training (OMMT), you can claim for any member of staff.

See more about claiming for OMMT.

You can only claim reimbursement towards the cost of the training itself, not extras like travel expenses.

How much you can claim

Every course or qualification has a maximum reimbursement amount per learner. This may be less than the training actually cost.

This does not apply to claims for in-house OMMT, which has a fixed reimbursement amount.

See more about how we calculate reimbursement amounts.

Submitting claims

To submit a claim you’ll need to include:

  • what the training was
  • who completed it
  • evidence of payment
  • evidence of training completion for each learner

See more information about what evidence you need to provide.

When you'll get paid

We aim to process your claim within 2 to 6 weeks. You’ll usually receive payment no more than 2 weeks after your claim is approved.

Who can register

To register, your organisation first needs to decide who is going to be the Senior Responsible Officer (SRO).

The SRO is the only person who can register with this service.

Who is the SRO?

The SRO is a senior member of staff who takes responsibility for ensuring that your organisation follows all the scheme’s legal requirements and submits accurate, non-fraudulent claims.

To register, the SRO must sign a grant determination letter for every financial year they wish to submit claims for. This is a legally binding document that outlines all the scheme’s terms and conditions (you’ll find it in the guide for employers).

They will also need to download the an authenticator app, such as Microsoft Authenticator, onto their mobile phone.

Before you start submitting claims, the SRO will need to provide your organisation's bank details so that we can reimburse you.

Who can submit claims?

The SRO can invite up to 9 other staff members to become ‘submitters’. Both the SRO and submitters will be able to submit and manage claims.

Register your organisation

To register your organisation, your SRO will need to provide the following information:

  • their name and contact details
  • their job title
  • your organisation’s workplace ID as it appears in the ASC-WDS
  • 2 different proof of address documents for your organisation (for example, a recent utility bill and a business rates bill)

If possible, we also request that they provide your organisation's:

  • Companies House registration number
  • VAT registration number

Once we have all the necessary information we'll verify it and carry out counter-fraud checks. We aim to do this as quickly as possible.

For more information about how we use your data, see our privacy notice.
